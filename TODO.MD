# kqring: what's open

Known limits are in [BENCHMARK.MD](BENCHMARK.MD#known-gaps) and [Differences from Linux](README.md#differences-from-linux). This is the work that could close some of them; [ROADMAP.MD](ROADMAP.MD) puts it in order.

## Performance

- **Cached ZFS reads, `psync` still ahead at 8 jobs.** With one read per enter kqring is 9% behind at 4 jobs and 11% at 8; with 4 to 16 reads per enter it's ahead up to 4 jobs and 2% behind at 8. What's left is the per-call path, spread thinly over about 25 functions, and fio's io_uring engine ([docs/worker-wakeups.md](docs/worker-wakeups.md#where-the-rest-of-the-gap-is-and-batching)). A ZFS read that can't block would remove the guess about cached reads but not raise throughput ([docs/async-zfs-reads.md](docs/async-zfs-reads.md#measured-before-building)).
- **1 to 4 jobs lose up to 0.6% to the inline-read change.** No read runs in the enter there. What's left is spread over several small pieces (the count of threads in enters, the `inline_ok` check, the spinner's check, the second average), none of which alone made a measurable difference ([docs/worker-wakeups.md](docs/worker-wakeups.md#reads-in-the-submitting-thread)).
- **Fan-in at 2,048 connections trails kqueue.** On the Ryzen a multishot receive on a `DEFER_TASKRUN` ring is 8% slower than kqueue at 2,048 AF_UNIX connections (7% when kqueue reads each connection into a buffer of its own) and 2% at 1,024, and ahead at 512; a default ring is ahead at 2,048. There the working set outgrows the L3, and each message brings 1.4 more lines from memory than kqueue's: the provided buffer and its ring entry, the request's three lines and its SQE, and on AF_UNIX the end-of-input knote ([docs/fan-in.md](docs/fan-in.md#what-stops-it-at-2048)).
- **A receive that waits costs about 210 ns more than one that finds its data**, less the extra system call, on a `DEFER_TASKRUN` ring on the Ryzen (250 on the i5 before phase 17; io_uring: 651 ns there). It is spread over the second enter's system call and its own work, arming and disarming the socket, the ring's mutex three times and the taskwork, each a few tens of nanoseconds; no single piece is left to remove ([docs/fan-in.md](docs/fan-in.md#phase-17)).
- **A default ring's ping-pong is about 87 ns slower than a `DEFER_TASKRUN` ring's** on the Ryzen (from 260 before phase 18): the writer's return to user space runs the receive, through a module AST, where a `DEFER_TASKRUN` ring runs it in the enter that waits. The AST's own path (the kernel's dispatch, scheduling it, `sq_sx` again, queueing for it) is most of it ([docs/fan-in.md](docs/fan-in.md#phase-18)).
- **Multishot fan-in on a `DEFER_TASKRUN` ring is 1.3–2.8% slower at 16 and 256 connections** (2.1% and 1.8% in the final comparison) than before phase 18, which adds one predictable branch for each queued request there; a build without it had those rows level and others 3–8% slower, from where its code lay ([docs/fan-in.md](docs/fan-in.md#phase-18)).
- **A cached `READ` costs 174–180 ns more than `pread(2)`** on the i5 (196 before phase 19), and fio's one job reads 0.85 of `psync`. The rest is a request's own machinery, a few ns at a time, most of it first touches of cache lines the 4 KiB copy evicts: clearing a reused request, the file's and the ring's references, the SQE's copy, the free list, the enter's lock check ([docs/cached-reads.md](docs/cached-reads.md#what-stops-100-ns)).
- **An enter takes a reference to the ring's descriptor**, registered or not, where Linux's `fdget()` takes none in a single-threaded process and a registered ring takes none at all. kqring's registered-ring table is per process, and another thread may unregister a ring during an enter; a per-thread table, as Linux's, could drop it ([docs/cached-reads.md](docs/cached-reads.md#what-stops-100-ns)).
- **A completion left to a thread whose call then blocks without being interruptible**, or runs long without sleeping, still waits one to two ticks for the watcher to hand it to the kernel thread. Since phase 20 an interruptible sleep is interrupted, as Linux's task work interrupts it, and other threads' completions go to the kernel thread at once ([docs/task-work-latency.md](docs/task-work-latency.md#phase-20)).
- **What is left of an enter's cost** is spread over the system call's entry and exit, submission, the ring's descriptor (`fget`), `sq_sx` and about 20 small functions. One `NOP` per enter now costs no more above FreeBSD's floor than io_uring above Linux's on the same machine ([docs/per-request-cost.md](docs/per-request-cost.md#on-the-machine-that-runs-both)); single requests still trail kqueue on the Ryzen, where fan-in is level with it.
- **A cached read in the enter costs more than `pread(2)`.** On tmpfs, one fio job reads 0.83 of `psync` on the i5 (io_uring: 1.05 of Linux's) and 0.74 on the Ryzen: fio's engine enters once per read, and kqring's own functions take 190 ns of each ([docs/cached-reads.md](docs/cached-reads.md#where-the-time-of-one-read-goes)). With spare CPUs, workers read one job's 32 in flight 13% faster, on three CPUs.

## Compatibility

- **A new mapping over a registered buffer.** FreeBSD keeps a held page in its memory object when its range is unmapped, and an anonymous mapping that extends the object over the same offsets maps the page again, zeroed, so the buffer and the new memory can be one. Linux keeps them apart. It would take the VM letting go of held pages at unmap ([docs/held-buffers.md](docs/held-buffers.md#what-the-implementation-changed)).
- **A fixed read in flight during `fork(2)`** lands in the child's copy of the buffer, as the parent's buffer is held again at the fork; on Linux it lands in the parent's.

## Measurement

- **Run on UFS with a real disk, and on NVMe.** Storage results so far are from a SATA SSD with ZFS, and md(4) disks.
- **fio's cached ZFS reads at 250,000–330,000 IOPS.** Seen once on the Ryzen on 2026-11-09, with kqring at 1 and 2 jobs, where 890,000 is usual, and not reproduced since: not with or without `zpool sync` after writing the file, nor with the ARC target squeezed to 755 MB. It is not the dbuf cache that gave `kqring_bench`'s file group two speeds ([BENCHMARK.MD](BENCHMARK.MD#pipes-and-files-inline-or-through-a-worker)): fio's dataset has 4 KiB records, and its `--direct=1` reads are ZFS's uncached reads, which don't use that cache. `fio/scale.sh` writes the ARC hits, misses and size for each cached run to `arc.txt`.

- **A fuzzer run whose leak check waits on a ring never closed.** One 1,800 s `kqring_fuzz` run of seed 2020 on the Ryzen (phase 21's validation, about 1 in 41 runs on both hosts) failed its leak check: after its summary, one ring was never closed and kept 2 sockets, about 64 MB of wired memory and its allocations, with 5 workers in `READ` on a datagram socket, for the whole 3 minutes; all of it was freed when the process exited. The likeliest cause is the process's registered-ring table, which `unregister_rings()` clears without reporting a failure. The validation script now saves the process's files and kernel stacks when a run outlives its summary.

## Not planned

- **Zero-copy `SPLICE`.** `IORING_OP_SPLICE` needs a pipe at one end, so it can't express a direct file-to-socket transfer. Programs on FreeBSD use `sendfile(2)`, or `READ_FIXED` with `SEND_ZC`.
